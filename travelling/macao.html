Macao: A Quick Historical Escape

Macao is the perfect effortless day trip from Hong Kong. A short ferry ride or a bus across the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge swaps neon towers for pastel-coloured Portuguese plazas, historic ruins and world-famous egg tarts. It's compact enough to explore in a day, which makes it the cheapest cultural weekend break for exchange students.

Getting there

  • Ferry. Ferries to Macao leave from the Hong Kong-Macau Ferry Terminal in Sheung Wan and from Tsim Sha Tsui. The crossing takes about an hour. Check the operator timetables and book ahead for weekends and holidays, because popular sailings fill up.
  • Bridge bus. The Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge shuttle bus leaves from Hong Kong Port near the airport. It's a good option if you want to see the bridge itself, but you need to reach the port first, which adds a transfer.
  • Passport. Take your passport and your Hong Kong student ID. Many nationalities can enter Macao visa-free for short stays, but check the rules for yours before you go.
  • Last boat back. Note the last departure to Hong Kong and set a phone alarm. Missing it means an unplanned night in Macao.

What to see on foot

  • Senado Square is the heart of the old town, with wavy black-and-white paving and pastel colonial buildings.
  • Ruins of St. Paul's is the carved stone facade of a former church and the postcard shot of Macao. Go early to beat the crowds.
  • A-Ma Temple is one of the oldest temples in the city and sits on a hillside by the harbour.
  • Guia Fortress gives you a view over the city and is one of the best places to understand how the old and new parts fit together.
  • Taipa Village and Coloane are quieter, with small streets, cafés and a slower pace, and they're good for a second day.

What to eat

  • Portuguese egg tarts. Macao's version has a caramelised top and a flaky crust. Try a few and pick a favourite.
  • Pork chop buns. A crisp, juicy pork chop in a soft bun is cheap, filling and found at small shops around the old town.
  • Macanese food. This local cuisine mixes Portuguese and Chinese flavours, and it's worth one proper sit-down meal.
  • Street snacks. Almond cookies and beef jerky are sold near Senado Square, and many shops let you try a sample first.

Getting around and paying

  • Walk. The historic centre is small, and most sights are within 20 minutes of each other on foot.
  • Free casino shuttle buses. Many casino resorts run free shuttles from the ferry terminals and the border. You can use them as cheap transport without going inside to gamble, and you must be 21 to enter a casino.
  • Cash. Your usual apps and cards aren't reliable here. Bring cash, and note that Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted but you may get change in Macao patacas.
  • Public buses are cheap but can be crowded, and signs are in Chinese and Portuguese, so keep your map app handy.

Student tip

  • Start early. Take a morning ferry so you can see the old town before the tour groups arrive.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. The old town is hilly and paved with small stones.
  • Go midweek if your timetable allows, because weekends and public holidays are very busy.
