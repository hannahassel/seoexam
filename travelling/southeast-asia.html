Southeast Asia: Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines

Southeast Asia is cheap, warm and a short flight from Hong Kong. The practical advice is the same in all three countries below, so read this once.

Before you go

  • Flights and entry. Compare Skyscanner, Momondo or Kiwi.com and check baggage fees. Entry rules depend on your passport, so check each government's official page, and keep your Hong Kong student status valid for re-entry.
  • Grab is the ride-hailing app across the region. It shows the price before you ride, so you avoid haggling. For tuk-tuks and tricycles, agree the fare first.
  • Cash. Street food and markets are often cash only. ATMs add fees, so ask your bank and withdraw bigger amounts less often. EUR or USD are the easiest to exchange.
  • Data. An e-SIM with an Asia package covers all three countries.
  • Insurance. Make sure yours covers scooters, diving and extreme sports.
  • Heat and food. Drink plenty of water, use sunscreen, and eat at busy stalls.

Vietnam

If you have more than a long weekend, visit Vietnam. The food is cheap and brilliant, and the Ha Giang loop is practically mandatory for everyone now.

  • Plan four to six days. A weekend only gets you one city, and the loop alone takes several days. Fly into Hanoi for the north or Ho Chi Minh City for the south. Apply for any e-visa on the official portal.
  • Hanoi. Wander the Old Quarter, walk around Hoan Kiem Lake, and eat pho, bun cha and egg coffee.
  • Ha Giang loop. A multi-day route through steep mountain passes, rice terraces and villages. Most loops start in Ha Giang city, reached by sleeper bus from Hanoi. Join a tour with a local rider, check your insurance covers motorbikes, and pack a warm layer.
  • Elsewhere. Hoi An has lantern-lit streets and tailors who make clothes to measure. Da Nang has the beaches.
  • Shopping. Go with an empty suitcase. Markets sell coffee, silk and souvenirs, and you can bargain politely. Dong notes have many zeros, so count carefully.
  • Getting around. Overnight trains and buses save a night of accommodation. Cross roads slowly and steadily.

Thailand

For a crazy weekend, go to Bangkok. Flights are short and often cheap, and the food and nightlife are famous.

  • Plan a short stay. A Friday-night flight and a Sunday-night return gives you two full days. Bangkok has two airports, so check which one your flight uses. With a week, add Chiang Mai or the southern islands.
  • Skytrain and metro. The BTS and MRT beat the traffic, so book a hostel near a station. River boats are a cheap way to reach the temples.
  • Tuk-tuks. Tell the driver to take you to Khao San Road and you're in for an insane night. Agree the fare first, and ignore anyone offering a cheap tour.
  • Traffic. It can get really bad, so prepare mentally for a motorbike taxi. Wear the helmet.
  • Sights. Grand Palace and Wat Pho (cover shoulders and knees), Chatuchak Weekend Market, and Yaowarat for evening street food.
  • Food. Pad thai, pad kra pao, mango sticky rice and Thai milk tea. Say “not spicy” unless you're ready.
  • Scams. If someone says a site is closed and offers a tour, walk away. Watch your drinks on Khao San Road.

The Philippines

The white beaches of the Philippines are simply gorgeous, but the trip needs more planning. The flight to Manila is cheap, and you usually need a second flight or ferry to reach the islands.

  • Pick one area. Choose Palawan, Cebu or Boracay and stay several days. Aim for four to five days, not a weekend.
  • Flights and ferries. Cebu Pacific can get you to practically any island. For short distances, keep the ferry in mind, and book the domestic leg early.
  • Season. Roughly December to May is driest, while typhoons are likelier from about June to November. Weather can delay boats, so leave a buffer before your flight home.
  • Palawan. El Nido and Coron have cliffs, lagoons and island-hopping boat tours. Coron is known for wreck diving.
  • Cebu. Waterfalls, canyoneering and snorkelling with sardine shoals make it an easy first island.
  • Getting around. Outside the cities you'll use tricycles, vans and boats, so agree prices first. Many islands charge small environmental fees in cash.
  • Boats. Check for life jackets, avoid sailing in bad weather, and use a dry bag. Book island-hopping tours in town with a group, where they're cheaper.
