Taiwan: The Optimal Destination

Whatever you're looking for, you can find it in Taiwan. It has buzzing night markets with amazing street food, mountain hikes, hot springs and island beaches where you can swim with sea turtles. It's only a flight of about an hour and a half from Hong Kong, so you spend your weekend in Taiwan rather than in transit. That's why so many exchange students call it their most memorable long weekend.

Getting there and between cities

  • Fly to Taipei or Kaohsiung. Taipei is the capital in the north, and Kaohsiung is the port city in the south. Compare flights on Skyscanner or Kiwi.com and check baggage rules, because budget airlines charge extra for bags.
  • Take the high-speed train. You can connect the two cities by train in roughly an hour and a half to two hours, which means you can fly into one and out of the other.
  • Check entry rules. Many nationalities can enter Taiwan visa-free for short stays, but rules vary, so check the official page for your passport.
  • Mind the season. Typhoon season runs through the summer and early autumn, so check the forecast before you book a flight you can't change.

What to do in Taipei

  • Night markets. Shilin and Raohe are the best-known. Go hungry, and share dishes so you can try more.
  • Taipei 101. You can see it from almost everywhere, and the free view from nearby Elephant Mountain is better value than the ticketed one.
  • Jiufen. This old mountain town has lantern-lit alleys and tea houses, and it's an easy day trip from the city.
  • Hot springs. Beitou is on the metro line, and public baths there are cheap.

What to do in Kaohsiung and the south

  • Pier-2 Art Center is a former warehouse district full of murals, galleries and cafés by the harbour.
  • Lotus Pond has colourful temples you can walk between.
  • Cijin Island is a short ferry ride away, with a beach and a seafood street.
  • Xiaoliuqiu is a small island a little further out where you can snorkel and swim with sea turtles. Book your boat and any tour ahead of time.

Apps and getting around

  • Line. This is the app you'll need. You can chat, pay, book appointments and use the YouBike rental with it. The catch is that some features need a local phone number, so get an e-SIM that includes one.
  • Metro. Taipei and Kaohsiung both have clean, simple metros that accept a stored-value transit card, which you can buy at the stations.
  • YouBike. These rental bikes are good for short trips along the river paths.
  • Taxis are cheap compared to Hong Kong, but have your destination written in Chinese.
  • Cash is still king at night markets, so bring some.

Student tip

  • Eat at the markets. A full dinner of street food costs very little, and it's the best way to try local dishes.
  • Learn two phrases. A “thank you” and a “how much” go a long way.
  • Book trains early on weekends, when seats sell out.
