Mainland China: Shenzhen and Guangzhou

Shenzhen and Guangzhou are the easiest way to see mainland China from CityU. Both work as a day trip or a full weekend, prices are far lower than in Hong Kong, and the food is excellent. The main thing to sort out first is paperwork and payments, because the mainland works differently from the rest of Asia.

Before you go

  • Check your visa. Requirements depend on your passport and change often, so read the official Chinese embassy or consulate page, not a forum post.
  • Check your Hong Kong status. Make sure your student visa stays valid for re-entry, and carry your passport and student ID.
  • Set up your apps at home. Many Western apps, including Google services, don't work reliably across the border. Install AMap, DiDi, Alipay or WeChat Pay and a translation app before you cross, and link your foreign card with a small test payment.
  • Check your e-SIM. Some e-SIM packages let blocked apps work on the mainland and some don't. Read the package description before you buy.

Getting to Shenzhen

From Kowloon Tong, the East Rail line runs to the border crossings at Lo Wu and Lok Ma Chau. You clear immigration on both sides, and the Shenzhen Metro starts almost at the border. Allow extra time at weekends and on public holidays, because queues can be long.

What to do in Shenzhen

  • Shopping. Shenzhen's malls are huge, and the Dongmen pedestrian street is the classic cheap-shopping spot. Huaqiangbei is the famous electronics district.
  • Art and culture. OCT Loft is a creative park with cafés and galleries, and Dafen Village is known for its painters.
  • Outdoors. Shenzhen Bay Park is a good place for a sea breeze and a skyline view.
  • Food. Try hotpot, Cantonese roast meats and street snacks. Menus often have photos, so pointing works.

A day or weekend in Guangzhou

Guangzhou is further north, but the high-speed intercity train from West Kowloon Station takes about an hour. You clear mainland immigration in Hong Kong before boarding, so allow time at the station.

  • Dim sum. Guangzhou is the home of Cantonese dim sum, and old tea houses serve it cheaply. Go in the morning, when the choice is widest.
  • History. Shamian Island has colonial-era buildings and quiet tree-lined streets, and Chen Clan Ancestral Hall shows traditional Lingnan craft.
  • Skyline. Canton Tower looks best after dark.
  • Markets. Beijing Road and the surrounding streets are full of student-budget stalls and snack shops.

Getting around and paying

  • DiDi is your best friend for ride hailing, and taxis are cheap, so you'll probably take more than you planned.
  • Metro systems in both cities are clean and simple, with English signs and station names.
  • Cash is less common than in Hong Kong. Rely on Alipay or WeChat Pay, but keep some cash for emergencies.
  • Bags are checked at metro stations. Pack light and leave large items at your accommodation.

Student tip

Bring an empty bag. Prices are low and you'll want to shop.
