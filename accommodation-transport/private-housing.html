Renting Private Housing in the Urban Core

For students who prefer freedom, independence and a shorter commute, renting private housing in areas like Kowloon Tong, Mong Kok or Prince Edward is a popular alternative. It requires navigating local real estate agents, security deposits and utility bills independently. In return, it places you right in the heart of the city, saves hours of weekly travel time, and offers a more immersive local living experience. The cost is around HK$6,000–8,000 a month, but depending on the arrangement and the neighbourhood it can vary a lot.

Where to look

Three areas come up again and again for CityU students.

  • Kowloon Tong is the closest to campus. CityU is next to the station, and the area is quieter and more residential, with Festival Walk nearby.
  • Mong Kok is busy, loud and full of food, shops and markets. It's one of the liveliest districts in Kowloon.
  • Prince Edward is central, with good MTR connections, so you can reach most of the city quickly.
  • If you don't mind the commute, you can look at places on Hong Kong Island.

How to find a room

The most effective way to find cheap rooms is to use the groups on social platforms, or to follow the guidelines the university provides. Flats are incredibly expensive in Hong Kong, but you can catch great deals if you keep your eyes open.

  • Join housing groups on social platforms early, and check them every day, because good rooms go quickly.
  • Follow the university's guidelines for housing advice and listings before you start searching.
  • Use local agents if you need to, but ask what fees they charge before you agree to a viewing.
  • Consider a shared flat. Renting a single room is usually much cheaper than a whole flat.
  • Ask other exchange students who have already been through the process.

What to check before you sign

  • View in person, or ask a friend in Hong Kong to go for you. If you can't, ask for as many photos and videos as possible. Don't pay anything before you've seen the room.
  • Check the size. Hong Kong rooms are small, and sizes are often given in square feet, so ask for exact measurements.
  • Look for damp and ventilation. Hong Kong is humid, so check the windows, the walls and the air conditioning.
  • Read the contract. Make sure the lease length suits your semester, and ask what happens if you leave early.
  • Ask about the deposit, how much it is and when you get it back.
  • Ask what bills cost. Electricity, water, gas and internet are often separate from the rent, so ask what a typical month looks like.

Costs to plan for

Private housing means more than the monthly rent.

  • Security deposit. Landlords usually ask for a deposit when you sign.
  • Agent fees. If you use an agent, there may be a commission.
  • Utility bills. Plan for electricity, water, gas and internet.
  • Furniture and basics. Check what's included in the flat, and budget for anything you need to buy.
  • Moving in. Bring only what you need, because space is tight.

Avoiding scams

  • Be wary of rooms that seem too cheap. If a price is far below similar listings, ask why.
  • Never transfer money before a viewing, and never to an account you can't verify.
  • Check who you're dealing with. Ask the agent for their licence details, and make sure the person who signs the lease is the landlord or an authorised agent.
  • Get everything in writing, including the deposit amount, the rent, the lease dates and what the rent covers.
  • Ask for advice. If something feels wrong, talk to your exchange coordinator or the university before you pay.

Settling in

  • Meet your neighbours and ask about the building's rules for noise, rubbish and laundry.
  • Learn your commute. Walk the route to campus once before term starts, and note which MTR exit you need.
  • Keep your documents safe. Save a copy of your tenancy agreement and your receipts.
  • Stay social. You won't have built-in events, so join a society or meet other exchange students early.

Is private housing right for you?

Choose private housing if you want your own routine and more privacy, you'd rather spend less time on the train, and you're comfortable handling contracts and bills. Choose the student compound if you want the lowest cost, built-in friends and no paperwork.
