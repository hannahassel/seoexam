The Handbook to Student Housing and Public Transit in Hong Kong

For incoming exchange students, the two main options for accommodation are the student compound in Ma On Shan or arranging your own private housing in the city. Both have their advantages and disadvantages, so start thinking about your preferences well before arrival.

  • Ma On Shan student compound → HK$12,500 per semester, about 45 minutes to campus and an instant community.
  • Renting private housing → Kowloon Tong, Mong Kok or Prince Edward: more independence, a shorter commute and higher costs.
  • The Octopus card → The card you need for transport and shops, the student version and the fare subsidy.
  • MTR (Mass Transit Railway) → Fast trips across the city, the right exit and the rules on board.
  • Ding dings (trams) → Cheap, scenic tram rides on Hong Kong Island.
  • Buses and minibuses → Beaches, trails and everywhere the trains don't reach, plus how to get off a minibus.
  • Taxis → Red, green and blue taxis, and when a taxi is worth it.

Getting around: which transport for which trip

Transportation in Hong Kong is well developed, and every part of the city is accessible with some form of transport. This overview shows which option to use for which trip, so you can move around without wasting time or money.

Night buses, ferries and the airport

  • Night buses. Some bus routes run after the MTR closes, which is useful after a night out. Check the route times in a bus app before you rely on them.
  • Star Ferry. The ferry between Tsim Sha Tsui and Hong Kong Island is cheap, takes Octopus, and gives you one of the best harbour views in the city.
  • Arriving at the airport. Plan your first journey in advance, and decide whether the MTR, a bus or a taxi suits your luggage best.

Getting around: quick tips

  • Use the MTR when you want speed, and the tram when you want sightseeing.
  • Plan with apps. HKeMobility offers live traffic news, the official MTR app shows the best exits, and Google Maps works fine for basic route planning.
  • Check the last train before a night out, and keep taxi money as a back-up.
  • Keep your Octopus topped up, so you never get stuck at a gate.

FAQ

How much does student housing cost in Hong Kong?
The Ma On Shan student residence costs HK$12,500 per semester. Private flats cost much more, and prices vary a lot by neighbourhood.
How long is the commute from Ma On Shan to CityU?
About 45 minutes each way by MTR, costing HK$10.7 per direction.
Should exchange students live in the residence or a private flat?
Choose the residence for budget and an instant social circle. Choose a private flat if you value independence and a short commute.
Do I need an Octopus card in Hong Kong?
Yes. It works on almost all public transport and in many shops, and the UnionPay version is good for travels to the mainland and Macao.
How do I get off a minibus in Hong Kong?
Call out “Yau lok, m goi!” or press the buzzer before your stop.
