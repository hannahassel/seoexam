Ma On Shan Student Compound

The student residence in Ma On Shan is the ultimate budget-friendly solution for your exchange semester. It costs HK$12,500 for one semester. There are additional costs, of course, but that applies to any option you choose. The catch is distance, because the campus is about 45 minutes away. Even so, a lot of students pick it, and it becomes the social hub of the semester.

Cost and commute

  • Rent: HK$12,500 for one semester.
  • Journey: roughly 45 minutes to campus and 45 minutes back.
  • Fare: HK$10.7 each direction.
  • Weekly cost: if you go to school and back five times a week, you'll spend about HK$107 on transport.
  • Reality check: most exchange students only have classes three or four days a week, so the real figure is usually lower. Check your timetable before you choose, and remember that early lectures mean early mornings.

Keep in mind that you cannot have overnight guests, so friends or family visiting you should book their own accommodation.

Community and social life

Living here guarantees built-in community events, instant neighbour friendships and a vibrant dorm culture.

  • Events. The competition between the halls gets super fun, and you'll want to take part in every one of them.
  • Friends. Your neighbours are other exchange students, so you meet people without trying.
  • Mornings. You'll need to master your alarm clock and the transit schedule to make morning lectures on time.
  • Nights out. On the way back from a night out, you can split the cost of a taxi with the others, because the car is guaranteed to be full.
  • Help. The floor RTs are always available for reporting and help, including in case of theft or other issues.

Rooms and shared spaces

The common room layout is the two-bed room, with a shared kitchen and bathroom for the whole floor. This makes private space hard to find, but you can go out to the garden and find a quiet spot at any time of the day.

  • Kitchen. Use depends on the floor residents, but you can usually cook without waiting for the stove.
  • Storage. The shared spaces have room for storage, so you don't have to keep everything in your own room.
  • Roommate. Be honest with yourself about how you feel about sharing, because you'll see your roommate every day.

What to buy after you move in

You'll need to do a little shopping after moving in. There's an arranged IKEA trip, or you can go by yourself whenever you feel like it. First, check whether the previous resident left anything behind. If you didn't bring anything from home, here's what you'll need:

  • Bedding: sheets, a cover and a pillow.
  • Kitchen: plates, a mug, a glass and cutlery.
  • Bathroom: towels, shower slippers, and a basket or dedicated bag for your bathroom essentials, so you can carry your stuff whenever you take a shower.
  • Laundry: detergent and a laundry bag or basket.
  • Extras: a small mirror, if you want to check what you look like before leaving the room.

Where to eat

If you don't feel like cooking, there are plenty of other options.

  • Double Cove Plaza is a five-minute walk from the dorm. It has options ranging from traditional Hong Kong food, through Thai, to sushi and Western food.
  • The MTR station has some small food stalls.
  • Supermarkets. There's a Taste store at the plaza and a Wellcome on the other side of the MTR station.
  • Wet market. Next to the Wellcome, you can buy fresh fruit, vegetables and meat.
  • You can bring back takeaway from the canteen for the cheapest solution.

Using the gym

The compound has a gym, which you can use once you've submitted a proof of eligibility.

  1. Submit a certificate from your home university, gym or sports community, or apply for the gym course here at CityU.
  2. Wait a couple of days for the process to be completed in the system.
  3. Collect a facility card. Go to the security counter, say you want to use the gym, and hand over your student ID. They'll write down your data and give you a card that opens the facility.
  4. Return the card when you're done, and take back your ID. Do this each time, and you can use the gym any time within the opening hours.

Is it right for you?

Choose the residence if you want the lowest cost, an instant social circle and no paperwork. Choose private housing if you want independence, more space and a shorter commute.
